# 0020. Explanations are stored and reviewed; chat answers are generated live with their sources

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-05
- Issue: #43 (Sprint 1 documentation)

## Context

The site shows two kinds of text about the Quran.

- **Reading content:** the explanation of each reading section, the meaning of each word in its verse, the surah introduction, and the prepared questions with their answers. These are the same for every reader, and they can be written ahead of time.
- **Answers to questions readers type**, which arrive in Sprint 2. Nobody can write these ahead of time, because nobody knows which questions will be asked.

Religious text that is wrong, or that uses the wrong word (the Christian term 하나님 for Allah, for example), changes the meaning of the verse for a reader who can't check the Arabic. So the question is which text is written and reviewed by people, and which is generated by a model.

## Decision

- **Reading content is stored and reviewed.** Explanations, word meanings, surah introductions and prepared questions are written by the project owner, kept as files in `content/`, and go through every review step before readers see them: draft, English approved, Korean drafted, Korean approved, published ([decision record: content in git, seeded into Supabase](0011-content-in-git-seeded-into-supabase.md)). They are never generated when a page loads, and generated text is never saved into them.
- **Chat answers are generated live, with their sources.** When a reader types a question, the question-answering service finds the passages in tafsir and hadith books that are closest to it, and a model writes the answer from those passages only.
  - Every answer cites the passages it uses, with the book and page, as source chips that open the book.
  - When the passages don't support an answer, the service declines and says so instead of guessing.
  - Answers are not reviewed, so they are labelled as generated and shown apart from the reviewed content.
  - Answers are not stored as content and never appear on the reading pages.

## Alternatives

- **Generate the explanations with a model and have reviewers correct them.** Faster to fill many surahs, but the reviewers would be checking a model's framing instead of writing from the sources, and the project's rule is that the owner writes all religious content.
- **Prepared answers only, with no chat.** Every answer would be reviewed, but readers could only ask the questions someone thought of in advance. The proposal's question panel is meant to answer the reader's own question.
- **Generated answers without sources.** Simpler, but the reader couldn't check anything, and nothing would show when the model is wrong. The proposal requires a source behind every explanation.

## Consequences

- The surah page stays fully reviewed. A reader can trust that everything on it passed the review steps.
- The chat can answer far more questions than the owner could ever prepare, but its answers can be wrong. The sources and the decline rule are what make them checkable. The decline rule is tested with a question the books don't cover.
- Generated answers must follow the same rule for Islamic terms as the content ([decision record: terms follow the Arabic word](0008-terms-follow-the-arabic-word.md)), so the service has to check them too.
- A good chat answer can't be promoted straight into content. If the owner wants it on a reading page, they write it as content and it goes through review like anything else.
- This extends the earlier choice to store prepared questions as reviewed content instead of mock AI answers ([decision record: fixed questions as stored content](0018-fixed-qa-as-stored-content.md)).
